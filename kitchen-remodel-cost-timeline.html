How Much Does a Kitchen Remodel Cost? 8 Factors and the Timeline

Quick answer

A mid-range kitchen remodel in Charlotte typically costs about $25,000 to $75,000, with cosmetic refreshes starting around $12,000 and high-end remodels passing $100,000. Most take 6 to 12 weeks of work once materials are on site.

Add 4 to 8 weeks before that for design, permits and ordering, since cabinets and counters usually set the start date. The biggest price drivers are scope, cabinets and whether the layout changes.

How much does a kitchen remodel cost is a question with a wide answer, because the word remodel covers everything from new paint and counters to a gutted room with a new layout. The good news is that the price of a kitchen remodel is predictable once you know which of a few decisions you are making, and those decisions are the same in almost every kitchen remodel we quote. Cabinets, counters and layout changes account for most of the money, and they also decide how long the kitchen remodel takes.

This guide walks through the eight factors that move the price, the typical Charlotte ranges for each, and what to expect week by week once the work starts. If you are weighing a smaller project first, our guide on how to paint kitchen cabinets covers a refresh that costs a fraction of a remodel.

1. Scope: refresh, remodel or full renovation

Scope is the single biggest factor in kitchen remodel cost. A refresh keeps cabinets and layout and replaces surfaces. A mid-range remodel replaces cabinets and counters in the same footprint. A full renovation changes the layout, moves walls or plumbing, and often touches the rooms around the kitchen.

Deciding scope early saves money. Changing it midway, for example deciding to move the sink after cabinets are ordered, is one of the most expensive things that can happen on a kitchen job, because it reopens design, ordering and scheduling at once.

2. Cabinets

Cabinets are usually the largest line in a kitchen budget, often about a third of the total. Price depends on construction, door style and whether they are stock, semi-custom or custom. Stock cabinets are the most affordable, semi-custom adds sizing and finish options, and custom costs the most.

If your cabinet boxes are sound and the layout works, painting or refacing can cut this line dramatically. Our guide on whether it is cheaper to paint cabinets or replace them walks through that decision, and our kitchen cabinet painting service handles it end to end.

3. Countertops

Countertops typically make up about 10 to 15 percent of a kitchen remodel. Laminate is the most affordable, quartz and granite sit in the middle, and marble and specialty stones cost the most. Price is driven by material, edge profile, cutouts and square footage.

Counters are also a timeline factor. They are templated after the base cabinets are installed and then fabricated, which usually takes one to three weeks. That gap is built into every realistic schedule, and it is when many homeowners feel the kitchen has stalled.

4. Layout changes, plumbing and electrical

Keeping the sink, range and refrigerator in place is the single best way to control cost. Moving them means rerouting water lines, drains, gas and electrical circuits, and sometimes opening floors or walls. That work adds real money and weeks to the schedule.

Older Charlotte homes often need electrical updates during a remodel, because modern kitchens need more dedicated circuits than older panels were built for. It is better to plan for that at the start than discover it when the walls are open.

5. Appliances

Appliances usually account for about 10 to 20 percent of the budget, and the range is wide. A standard package costs a fraction of a professional-style range, a built-in refrigerator and a panel-ready dishwasher. Choose appliances before cabinets are finalized, since cabinet sizes depend on them.

Appliance lead times can also affect the schedule. If a specific model is backordered, the kitchen may be finished but waiting. Ordering early, or choosing in-stock models, keeps the job moving.

6. Flooring, backsplash and walls

Flooring, backsplash tile, drywall repair and paint are smaller lines individually but add up. Tile choice matters most: simple subway tile is affordable, while handmade tile, large-format slabs or complex patterns cost more in both material and labor.

These finishes come last in the sequence, which is why they are easy to underbudget. Our tile work, hardwood flooring and drywall repair crews often handle this stage in one visit so trades are not waiting on each other.

7. Permits and inspections

Structural, plumbing, electrical and gas work in Charlotte generally needs a permit through Mecklenburg County Code Enforcement, with inspections at key stages such as rough-in and final. Cosmetic work such as paint, cabinet painting and replacing counters in place usually does not need a permit at all.

Permits add some cost and a little time, but they protect you at resale and make sure the work is done to code. A contractor should pull them as part of the job rather than leave it to you.

8. Labor, lead times and contingency

Labor usually makes up a large share of a kitchen budget, and the schedule depends on how well trades are sequenced. A good plan lines up demolition, rough-in, inspections, cabinets, counters and finishes so nobody is standing around.

Always hold a contingency of 10 to 20 percent. Opening walls in an older house can reveal water damage, outdated wiring or uneven framing. A contingency means those discoveries are an inconvenience, not a crisis.

What to expect during a kitchen remodel timeline

A typical mid-range kitchen follows this sequence once materials are on site:

  1. Demolition and protection, week 1. Old cabinets and counters come out, and dust protection goes up.
  2. Rough-in and inspections, weeks 1 to 3. Plumbing, electrical and any framing changes, followed by inspections.
  3. Drywall and paint prep, weeks 2 to 4. Walls are repaired and prepared.
  4. Cabinet installation, weeks 3 to 5. Base and wall cabinets go in and are leveled.
  5. Countertop templating and fabrication, weeks 4 to 7. Counters are measured, cut and installed.
  6. Backsplash, flooring and finishes, weeks 6 to 9. Tile, trim, hardware and paint.
  7. Appliances, punch list and cleanup, weeks 8 to 12. Final hookups and touch-ups.

Before any of that, allow 4 to 8 weeks for design, permits and ordering. How long does a kitchen remodel take end to end? Usually 10 to 20 weeks from first meeting to finished kitchen, depending on lead times.

How to budget for a kitchen remodel

Start with the number you are comfortable spending, then subtract a 10 to 20 percent contingency before you choose anything. Spend first on the things that are hard to change later, such as layout, cabinets and plumbing, and save on items that are easy to upgrade later, such as lighting fixtures and hardware.

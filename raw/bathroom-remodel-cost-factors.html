A powder room refresh can start around $4,000, while a primary bath with layout changes often runs $30,000 to $60,000 or more.

How much does a bathroom remodel cost is harder to answer than it looks, because two bathrooms the same size can be thousands of dollars apart. The difference is almost never the square footage. It is the handful of decisions about layout, shower and tile that set the scope of the bathroom remodel before anyone picks a paint color.

This guide focuses on those decisions: the seven factors that move the price of a bathroom remodel, what each one tends to cost, and where the 2027 bathroom trends are heading. 1. Size and type of bathroom

The type of bathroom matters as much as its size. A powder room has a toilet and sink and no wet area, so it is the least expensive to update. A hall bath adds a tub or shower. A primary bath often has a double vanity, a larger shower, and sometimes a separate tub, all of which multiply cost.

Size still matters for materials, especially tile. But a small bathroom with a tiled shower and a new layout can cost more than a larger one that keeps everything in place, which is why the next factor is the one to watch.

2. Whether the layout changes

Keeping the toilet, shower and sink where they are is the single best way to control bathroom remodel cost. Moving any of them means rerouting drains and water lines, and sometimes cutting into the floor or subfloor, which adds labor, permits and time.

Moving a toilet in particular is expensive, because the drain has to slope correctly to the main line. Layout changes can add several thousand dollars before a single tile goes down, but they are often what makes a small or awkward bathroom work.

3. Shower, tub or both

The wet area is usually the largest line in a bathroom budget. A replacement tub and surround is the most affordable option. A tiled walk-in shower costs more, because of waterproofing, a proper pan and tile labor. A freestanding tub adds the price of the tub plus plumbing for its location.

Walk in tub installation in Charlotte is a specialized job, often for accessibility, and it usually needs electrical work for heated or jetted models. It costs more than a standard tub but can let homeowners stay in their house longer.

4. Tile choice and layout

Tile affects both material and labor costs. Simple ceramic subway tile is affordable and quick to install. Large-format porcelain, natural stone and handmade tile cost more to buy and take more time to lay, and intricate patterns such as herringbone add labor again.

Tile layout matters too. A full-height tiled shower costs more than a wainscot, and a tiled niche adds a little work but saves on shelving. Our tile work crew can help you balance the look you want against the price.

5. Vanity and countertop

The vanity is where a bathroom budget can flex the most. Stock vanities with an integrated top are the most affordable, semi-custom adds sizing and finish options, and custom vanities with stone tops cost the most. A double vanity doubles the sinks, faucets and plumbing.

Vanity installation in Charlotte is quick if the plumbing stays in place, and it is one of the most visible upgrades in the room. Floating, wall-mounted vanities are popular, but they need solid blocking in the wall, which is easiest to add while the walls are open.

6. Flooring

Bathroom flooring installation in Charlotte is usually tile or luxury vinyl. Tile is durable and traditional, and it can be heated. Luxury vinyl is waterproof, warmer underfoot and less expensive to install. The subfloor matters: soft or damaged subfloor near a toilet or tub must be repaired before new flooring goes down.

Heated floors add cost but are worth considering in a primary bath. They need to be planned before the tile goes down, since the mat or cable sits under the floor.

7. Fixtures, ventilation and permits

Faucets, showerheads, lighting and the exhaust fan round out the budget, along with any permits the plumbing and electrical work needs. Fixture prices vary widely, and low-flow models certified by the EPA's WaterSense program use noticeably less water without feeling weak in the shower.

Ventilation deserves more attention than it gets. Charlotte summers are humid, and a bathroom without a properly sized, vented exhaust fan will grow mildew and peel paint. Plumbing and electrical changes generally need a permit, which a contractor should pull as part of the job.

Bathroom trends for 2027

Bathroom trends 2027 lean warm and natural. Expect warm wood vanities, earthy greens and soft blues in step with the new 2027 paint colors of the year, curbless walk-in showers, large-format tile with fewer grout lines, and integrated lighting in mirrors and niches.

Accessibility is also moving into the mainstream, with wider doorways, grab bars that look like towel bars and curbless entries. These features make a bathroom easier to use at any age and appeal to more buyers.

The best time to remodel a bathroom in NC

The best time to remodel a bathroom in NC is often late fall through early spring. Contractor calendars tend to be less crowded after the summer rush, and cooler, drier air helps paint and some finishes cure. A project booked in fall can often start sooner than one booked in May.

How much does a bathroom remodel cost in Charlotte?

What is the most expensive part of a bathroom remodel?
Moving the toilet, shower or drain is usually the biggest single cost jump, followed by a tiled walk-in shower. Both add plumbing labor and waterproofing work on top of the finish materials.
Is a walk-in shower more expensive than a tub?
Usually, yes. A tiled walk-in shower needs waterproofing, a proper pan and tile labor, while a tub and surround is quicker to install. Walk-in showers are popular with buyers, which helps offset the cost.

Quick answer

To paint kitchen cabinets, remove the doors and hardware, degrease every surface, scuff sand, prime with a bonding primer, and apply two thin coats of a cabinet-grade enamel, then let the finish cure before rehanging the doors.

A professional cabinet paint job for an average Charlotte kitchen typically costs about $3,500 to $7,000, depending on the number of doors and drawers, the condition of the boxes, and whether the doors are sprayed off site.

Knowing how to paint kitchen cabinets well comes down to one idea: paint only sticks as well as the surface underneath lets it. Kitchen cabinets collect a film of cooking grease and hand oil that you cannot see, and most factory finishes are glossy and slick. Paint over either one and it will look good for a few months, then chip at every handle and edge.

1. Remove doors, drawers and hardware

Take every door and drawer front off, remove hinges, knobs and pulls, and label each piece with its location using painter's tape. Number the tape on the back of the door and on the matching spot inside the box so everything goes back where it came from.

Painting doors flat on a work surface gives a far smoother finish than painting them hanging, and it keeps paint off the hinges. Store hardware in labeled bags. If you are replacing hardware, check that new pulls match the existing hole spacing, or plan to fill and redrill.

2. Degrease every surface

Clean doors, drawer fronts and cabinet boxes with a degreasing cleaner, paying extra attention to the area around the stove, the range hood and the pulls. Rinse with clean water, wipe dry, and let everything dry completely before any sanding, because grease smears into sandpaper and spreads.

This is the step people skimp on, and it is the most common reason cabinet paint fails. A TSP substitute or a dedicated cabinet degreaser works well. Wipe with a clean rag afterward and check it: if the rag still picks up a yellow film, clean again.

3. Repair dings, gaps and loose joints

Fill dents, scratches and old hardware holes with wood filler, and let it dry fully before sanding it flush. Re-glue any loose edges or applied trim, tighten hinge screws that have worked loose, and fix anything that wobbles on the cabinet boxes before paint locks it in.

Paint shows every flaw more than a stained finish does, especially in a satin or semi-gloss sheen under kitchen lighting. Taking ten minutes per door here is the difference between a finish that looks factory-made and one that looks painted over.

4. Scuff sand for adhesion

Lightly sand every surface with 180 to 220 grit paper or a sanding sponge to dull the gloss. You are not trying to remove the old finish, only to give primer a slightly rough surface to grip, so a few even passes on each face and edge is enough.

Can you paint kitchen cabinets without sanding? Mostly, yes. A liquid deglosser followed by a high-adhesion bonding primer works on many factory finishes. We still recommend a light scuff on high-wear areas like door edges and around the pulls, because that is where paint chips first.

5. Remove every trace of dust

Vacuum the surfaces, then wipe with a damp microfiber cloth or a tack cloth and let them dry. Sanding dust trapped under primer shows up as a gritty texture that no amount of topcoat will hide.

Clean the room as well as the cabinets. Dust that settles on wet paint from shelves, range hoods and floors is a common cause of a rough finish. Close vents and doors while you work if you can.

6. Prime with a bonding primer

Apply a bonding primer designed for slick surfaces, in one thin, even coat. On oak and other open-grain woods, a grain filler or a second coat of primer keeps the grain from telegraphing through the paint.

If the cabinets have knots or tannin bleed, or there are water stains under the sink, use a stain-blocking primer on those areas. Let the primer dry fully and sand lightly with fine grit before painting.

7. Caulk the gaps on the boxes

Run a thin bead of paintable caulk along seams where face frames meet walls or panels, and in any gaps between trim pieces. Smooth it with a damp finger and let it cure before painting.

Caulked seams make painted cabinets look intentional and built-in. Keep caulk off the doors themselves, where it can crack as the wood moves with Charlotte's humidity through the year.

8. Apply the first coat of enamel

Use a waterborne urethane or alkyd enamel made for cabinets and trim. Apply a thin coat with a quality brush and a fine foam roller, or spray it. Thin coats level smoother and cure harder than thick ones.

Wall paint stays soft and sticky on cabinets for months and marks easily. Cabinet enamels cost more per gallon but go a long way on doors, and they are what make the finish look smooth and hold up to daily use.

9. Apply the second coat and let it cure

Let the first coat dry for the time on the can, sand lightly with very fine grit, clean off the dust, and apply the second coat. Most cabinets need two finish coats for even color and full coverage.

Paint feels dry within hours, but most cabinet enamels take two to four weeks to fully cure and reach their hardness. Leave doors to dry flat for at least a day before handling, and longer in humid weather.

10. Rehang doors and reinstall hardware carefully

Once the finish is hard enough to handle, reinstall the hardware and rehang doors in their labeled positions. Adjust the hinges so the gaps between doors are even and every door closes without rubbing, because rubbing is what wears through fresh paint at the edges first.

Use soft bumper pads on the inside of doors and drawer fronts so painted surfaces do not stick to the boxes while they finish curing. Be gentle for the first few weeks: avoid harsh cleaners and hanging damp towels on the doors.

DIY or hire a crew?

DIY works if you have time, a clean space to lay doors flat, and patience for thin coats. The results depend almost entirely on prep and on how smooth you can get the finish.

A professional kitchen cabinet painting crew brings spray equipment, a controlled drying space and a system for getting doors back without scuffs, which is why sprayed doors often look closer to factory than brushed ones. Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to paint kitchen cabinets professionally?
Typically about $3,500 to $7,000 for an average Charlotte kitchen. The number of doors and drawer fronts is the biggest driver, followed by the condition of the wood and whether doors are sprayed off site or brushed in place.
Can you paint kitchen cabinets without sanding?
Mostly. A liquid deglosser plus a high-adhesion bonding primer works on many factory finishes. A light scuff sand on door edges and around pulls is still worth doing, because those are the areas where paint chips first.
What kind of paint is best for kitchen cabinets?
A cabinet-grade enamel, such as a waterborne urethane or alkyd, in satin or semi-gloss. These level smoothly and cure hard. Regular wall paint stays soft, marks easily and tends to stick when doors close.
How long does painted cabinet finish take to cure?
It feels dry in a few hours, but most cabinet enamels take two to four weeks to reach full hardness. Handle doors gently in that period, use bumper pads, and avoid harsh cleaners.
Is painting cabinets cheaper than replacing them?
Usually by a wide margin. Painting an average kitchen costs a fraction of new cabinets, provided the boxes are solid and the layout works. If the boxes are damaged or you want a new layout, replacement or refacing makes more sense.

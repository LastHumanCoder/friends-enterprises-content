How Much Does Pressure Washing Cost? 6 Price Factors in Charlotte

Quick answer

How much pressure washing costs in Charlotte depends on the surface and its size. Washing a typical house exterior usually costs about $300 to $700, a driveway about $100 to $300, and a deck about $200 to $500. Many companies price siding at roughly $0.15 to $0.40 per square foot.

The six factors that move the price are the size of the area, the type of surface, how dirty it is, height and access, add-ons like gutters, and whether the job is bundled with painting or staining.

1. The size of the area

Size is the starting point for any quote. Siding is usually priced by the square foot of wall area, driveways and patios by the square foot of concrete, and decks by their surface area. A larger house or a longer driveway simply takes more time, water and cleaner to finish properly.

Most companies also set a minimum charge, because setup and travel take the same time whatever the size of the job. That is why washing only a small patio can cost nearly as much as a whole driveway, and why grouping several surfaces into one visit is usually better value.

2. The type of surface

Different surfaces need different methods, and method affects price. Concrete driveways and walkways can take high pressure and a surface cleaner. Vinyl, fiber-cement and painted siding need soft washing with low pressure and a cleaner. Wood decks, brick and roofs each need their own approach.

Soft washing is often priced differently from high-pressure cleaning because it uses more cleaning solution and more care. It is also the only safe way to clean most siding and roofs, since high pressure can force water behind siding, strip paint and damage shingles.

3. How dirty it is

Light dust and pollen wash off quickly. Heavy mildew, algae, oil stains, rust and red clay staining take pretreatment, more dwell time and sometimes specialty cleaners, all of which add time and cost to the job.

North-facing walls and shaded areas in Charlotte grow the most mildew, and a driveway under trees collects staining faster than one in full sun. If a surface has not been washed in several years, expect the first cleaning to cost more than regular maintenance washes afterward.

4. Height and access

A single-story ranch is quick to wash from the ground. Two-story homes, steep lots, tall chimneys and areas behind dense landscaping take longer and may need ladders, extension wands or a roof-safe setup, which adds time and raises the price of the job accordingly.

Access to water matters too. A crew needs a working outdoor spigot, and very large jobs may need more water flow than a standard house connection gives. Making sure spigots work and moving vehicles, furniture and planters before the crew arrives keeps the visit efficient.

5. Add-ons: gutters, sealing and stain removal

Gutter brightening, window rinsing, rust or oil stain removal and concrete sealing all add to the base price. Sealing a driveway or paver patio after washing protects it and keeps it cleaner longer, but it is a separate step with its own material cost.

Concrete pressure washing in Charlotte often includes a pretreatment for oil and red clay stains. Patio pressure washing and sidewalk pressure washing are commonly quoted together with the driveway, since they use the same equipment and method.

6. Bundling with painting or staining

Pressure washing before painting in Charlotte is a required step, not an extra. Paint and stain only bond to a clean surface, so washing is built into every good exterior job. Bundling the wash with the paint or stain work is usually cheaper than booking them separately.

The surface needs to dry fully after washing before paint or stain goes on, usually at least a day and longer in cool or humid weather. How to pressure wash a house safely

If you are doing it yourself, the most important rule is to use low pressure on siding. Use a wide-angle tip, such as 25 or 40 degrees, keep the wand at least a couple of feet from the surface, and let a house-wash cleaner do the work of killing mildew rather than blasting it off.

Wash from the bottom up to apply cleaner, then rinse from the top down. Never aim upward under lap siding, where water can get behind the boards, and never aim at windows, vents, light fixtures or electrical outlets. Cover plants near the house and rinse them before and after, since cleaners can damage foliage.

Pressure washers can cause serious injuries, so wear eye protection and closed shoes, and never point the wand at people or pets. Wash water can also carry cleaners and dirt into storm drains, which is why the EPA's guidance on nonpoint source pollution is worth a read before you choose a cleaner.

Frequently asked questions

How much does pressure washing cost in Charlotte?
A typical house exterior costs about $300 to $700, a driveway about $100 to $300, and a deck about $200 to $500. Siding is often priced at about $0.15 to $0.40 per square foot. Size, surface, grime and height are what move the price.
Is pressure washing or soft washing better for siding?
Soft washing. It uses low pressure and a cleaner that kills mildew, so it cleans siding without forcing water behind it or stripping paint. High pressure is for hard surfaces like concrete driveways and walkways.
How often should you pressure wash a house in Charlotte?
Every one to two years for most homes. Shaded walls, homes under trees and houses near red clay may need it more often. Washing before painting is always required, whatever the schedule.
Should you pressure wash before painting?
Yes. Paint only bonds to a clean surface, so washing is part of every good exterior paint job. Let the siding dry fully, usually at least a day, before primer or paint goes on.
Can pressure washing damage a house?
It can if the pressure is too high or aimed wrongly. High pressure can strip paint, gouge wood, break window seals and force water behind siding. Using low pressure and the right cleaner avoids that.

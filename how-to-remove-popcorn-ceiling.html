How to Remove Popcorn Ceiling: 8 Steps and What It Costs

Quick answer

To remove popcorn ceiling texture, test it for asbestos if the house was built before the mid-1980s, cover the room in plastic, mist a small section with water, let it soak for 10 to 15 minutes, and scrape it off with a wide taping knife. Then repair, skim coat, prime and paint the ceiling.

Removal alone typically runs about $1 to $2 per square foot in most Charlotte homes. With skim coating and painting included, expect roughly $2.50 to $5 per square foot.

Learning how to remove popcorn ceiling texture is mostly learning how to control the mess and finish the ceiling underneath. The scraping itself is quick on an unpainted popcorn ceiling: the texture softens with water and falls away in sheets. What decides whether the room looks finished or patchy is everything after the scraper: the repairs, the skim coat and the paint.

This guide walks through the full job in order, with typical Charlotte costs, the asbestos question every older popcorn ceiling raises, and an honest look at when DIY makes sense. 1. Test for asbestos if the home is older

If your home was built before the mid-1980s, have a sample of the ceiling texture tested before you disturb it. Asbestos was used in spray-on acoustic ceilings for decades, and the only way to know is a lab test from an accredited lab.

A single sample test is usually a modest cost compared with the job. If the result is positive, do not scrape it yourself. Asbestos removal in North Carolina is a licensed, regulated trade, and the EPA's asbestos guidance for homeowners explains why disturbing it is the risk. A positive test does not always mean removal: in some rooms, covering the ceiling with new drywall is the safer and cheaper route.

2. Clear and seal the room

Empty the room, switch off the HVAC so dust does not travel, and cover the floor and walls in plastic sheeting taped at the top. Remove or cover light fixtures and ceiling fans, and turn off the breaker for the room.

Wet popcorn texture is heavy and sticky, and it gets everywhere. Many homeowners run the plastic up the walls and tape it just below the ceiling line so the whole room becomes a tent. Put a layer of rosin paper over the floor plastic so it is not slippery when wet debris lands on it.

3. Check whether the texture has been painted

Scrape a small test patch dry, then mist another and wait. If water soaks in and the texture softens, it is unpainted and will come off easily. If water beads and nothing softens, it has been painted, which changes the job.

Painted popcorn ceiling is the main reason DIY removals stall. The paint seals the texture, so water cannot reach it. Options are a chemical texture remover, very patient scraping, or skipping removal and skim coating over the texture. This is the point where many homeowners call a crew.

4. Mist the texture and let it soak

Use a pump garden sprayer to mist a section of about 4 by 4 feet. Do not soak it. Too much water can loosen the drywall tape and soften the paper face of the board underneath, which creates repair work later.

Wait 10 to 15 minutes, then test with the knife. If the texture resists, mist lightly again and wait a few more minutes. Working in small sections keeps the area you are scraping at the right softness instead of drying out before you reach it.

5. Scrape with a wide knife or ceiling scraper

Hold a 6 to 12 inch taping knife or a dedicated ceiling scraper at a low angle and push it along the ceiling. Round the corners of the knife slightly with a file so they do not gouge the drywall paper.

Work in overlapping passes and keep going section by section. A scraper with a bag attachment catches much of the debris before it hits the plastic. Take breaks: this is overhead work, and tired arms are when gouges happen.

6. Repair, then skim coat the ceiling

Once the texture is off, the ceiling underneath is rarely smooth. Builders rarely finished drywall carefully under popcorn, so expect nail pops, visible tape seams, gouges and patches of torn paper, all of which need repair before a flat finish looks right under ceiling lights.

Seal any torn paper with a stain-blocking primer before applying joint compound, or it will bubble. Then apply thin coats of joint compound across the whole ceiling. Learning how to skim coat walls and ceilings takes practice: several thin coats sanded between each are easier to get right than one thick one. 7. Sand, prime and decide on the finish

Sand the dried compound smooth with a pole sander, wipe off the dust, and prime the whole ceiling with a drywall primer. Primer seals the fresh compound so the topcoat dries evenly instead of flashing, which is when patched areas show through as dull or shiny spots.

At this stage you can choose a smooth ceiling or a new texture. Knockdown vs orange peel texture is the usual choice: orange peel is a fine, even spatter that hides minor flaws, while knockdown is a heavier spatter flattened with a trowel for a subtle, stucco-like look. Both are far less dated than popcorn and hide more imperfections than a smooth finish.

8. Paint the ceiling

Finish with a flat ceiling paint in two coats, rolling in one direction per coat and switching direction for the second. Flat paint hides minor surface variation that any sheen would show up under a ceiling light or the low winter sun coming through a window.

Wait for the primer to dry fully, and keep a wet edge as you roll so you do not leave lap marks. If you are painting the walls too, do the ceiling first. Our interior painting crew typically handles ceiling and walls together once the removal is finished.

DIY vs hire a pro

DIY makes sense for one small room with unpainted texture that tests clear, if you are comfortable with overhead work and drywall finishing. The scraping is easy. The finishing is where amateur jobs show.

Hire a pro when the texture is painted, the ceilings are high, the house has several rooms to do, or you want a smooth ceiling with no visible seams. A crew that does drywall repair every week will skim and sand a ceiling faster and flatter than most homeowners manage on a first attempt. Frequently asked questions

How much does popcorn ceiling removal cost?
Typically about $1 to $2 per square foot for removal alone and $2.50 to $5 per square foot with skim coating, priming and paint. A 12 by 12 foot room usually lands between $360 and $750 fully finished, assuming unpainted texture that tests clear for asbestos.
Is it safe to remove popcorn ceiling myself?
It can be, if the texture tests negative for asbestos and you protect the room properly. Homes built before the mid-1980s should always have a sample tested first. If it tests positive, leave it to a licensed abatement contractor or cover it with new drywall.
How do you remove popcorn ceiling that has been painted?
Painted texture does not soften with water, so it usually needs a chemical texture remover or slow dry scraping. Many homeowners skip removal and skim coat over it instead. This is the most common point where DIY jobs are handed to a crew.
What is the difference between knockdown and orange peel texture?
Orange peel is a fine, even spatter that looks like the skin of an orange and hides small flaws. Knockdown is a heavier spatter flattened with a trowel for a subtle, textured look. Both hide more imperfection than a smooth ceiling and look far less dated than popcorn.
How long does it take to remove a popcorn ceiling?
Scraping a single room takes a few hours. Repairs, skim coats, drying time, sanding, priming and paint usually stretch a room to two or three days, because each coat of compound needs to dry before the next.
